# Map-data pipeline

The integrated regional generator derives its original-patch exclusion from the active manifest through `san-diego-region-geometry.mjs`, rather than hard-coding the smaller pre-expansion bounds. Only regional outputs are regenerated; the original GLB, manifest, layout, source and population remain unchanged.

These scripts generate tracked road, park, waterfront, ambient-layout, address, and CityGML runtime assets. Preserve deterministic placement, local coordinate systems, road/footprint validation, content versions, and merged interactive landmark geometry. Run only the generator needed during iteration.

## Direct files

- `build-ambient-layout.mjs` generates validated deterministic New York vegetation and ambient placement.
- `build-hudson-waterfront.mjs` derives Hudson waterfront geometry.
- `build-manhattan-model-data.mjs` prepares the Manhattan overview model dataset.
- `build-manhattan-overview.mjs` derives one noninteractive Meshopt-compressed Manhattan overview from the exact 405 tiles previously loaded inside the 2,600-meter eager radius and Hudson cutoff. It preserves every wall/roof triangle and source 8-bit palette, omits buried grounds and normal buffers, welds identical position/color vertices, and batches buildings into two draws. Six omitted landmark bases come from exact `runtime.details` rings/holes and the active wall/roof palette; the seventh detail record already exists in the tiles and is not duplicated. The exact Bobst/arch source models use the runtime's fitted transforms and material policy. All eight tile-omitted landmarks are therefore present before interactive details load, with linear colors rounded to 8-bit RGB. Its 16-bit geographic position grid has a checked maximum bake-plus-quantization displacement below 7 cm. A third batch retains the exact existing Washington/Union/context park grass rings at 0.74 m; the loader must restore the documented road-stencil material state. It also extracts only the exact landmark `details` object into `details.json`; the offline full runtime metadata and original tiles remain immutable. Run `node scripts/map-data/build-manhattan-overview.mjs` after changing city tiles, the fitted models, park source rings or generated ambient context rings; it writes only the overview GLB, its provenance/measurement manifest and compact details JSON, with deterministic hashes and no network access.
- `build-manhattan-parks.mjs` prepares Manhattan park polygons.
- `build-nyc-data.mjs` acquires and transforms lower Manhattan geographic data.
- `build-san-diego-car-spawns.mjs` separately generates deterministic San Diego ordinary-car routes from the final mapped roads, with full vehicle clearance, school concentration, sparse neighborhoods, bridge exclusions, and no taxis. It is independent of New York traffic and pedestrian placement.
- `build-san-diego-people-spawns.mjs` generates deterministic San Diego pedestrian movement envelopes concentrated near school buildings, with sparse walkers on mapped neighborhood paths. It checks ground, road, building, tree, and campus-detail clearance against the final scene.
- `build-san-diego.mjs` generates static Torrey Highlands geography and GLB assets using the ignored upstream Map3D checkout and public geographic sources. Its palette uses sage terrain, richer park greens, dry coastal scrub, cream stucco, and terracotta roofs, while roads and parking retain paved colors. Source provenance, projection assumptions, and the exact palette accompany its outputs; ordinary regeneration reuses the checked-in source snapshot. It emits overview zoom 0.5 and a 240-meter northward target shift to keep Westview, the plaza, and Mesa Verde in view, matching the checked-in manifest. Mesa Verde’s reference-based north-south oval track, green infield, and athletics lawn are baked into the selectable Mesa Verde mesh as adjacent surfaces with holes to avoid stacking additive glow inside the track; its field excludes supplemental trees. All four landmark complexes are removed from the background building batch and merged with their roof/facade details into individual selectable roots. The manifest records each school’s mapped source IDs, merged bounds, and camera preset. School-specific detailing adds cream stucco, subdued roof colors, roof-edge trim, height-relative framed windows, fully contained vents, doors and supported entrance canopies to the seven Mesa Verde and twelve Deer Canyon mapped buildings. Verified open campus areas receive modest courts, paths, supported shade structures and seating; Deer Canyon also receives a play surface. These artistic approximations merge into each school root and publish exact placements/counts in manifest and layout metadata. Concave-safe footprint checks reject roof, parcel, building and road conflicts; every authored ground footprint excludes trees.
- `build-san-diego-region.mjs` expands San Diego from Solana Beach/Fairbanks Ranch to UCSD/Miramar and east through Carmel Mountain/Scripps Ranch. It emits a separate coast-and-canyon GLB, district cameras, a compact creek waterfall with low grounded rock outcrops and a static annotation, audit layout, source-coverage rectangle and measured content-height range without modifying the original Torrey Highlands assets. Torrey Pines has a raised coastal bluff scaled to fit its mapped roads and an oblique camera; its warm cliff facets and all existing map layers use the same locally refined terrain triangles. Buildings have terrain-following foundations and level roofs; roads taper into bridges and the original patch. The coastal main road stays low beside the beach/lagoon, while a separate connected Park Road profile climbs into the reserve; exact mapped bends and narrower local paths match the reference geography. Landcover retains the original semantic palette, and planting excludes mapped buildings, streets, water and recreation surfaces. Use `npm run data:san-diego-region`; ordinary builds are offline.
- `build-san-diego-overview.mjs` derives the noninteractive whole-city proxy from the exact, hash-verified original and regional GLBs. `npm run data:san-diego-overview` writes only `overview.glb` and `overview-manifest.json`. It preserves all source node transforms and source Float32 positions/colors, drops normal buffers for Three.js shader-derived flat shading, welds equal position/color vertices, keeps upward-facing building roofs/campus surfaces, and compacts meshoptimizer's reduced index/vertex buffers. Terrain has a 0.005 m simplifier error limit; roads/landcover/beach/creeks use 0.03 m; roof interiors use 0.5 m with locked boundaries; trees use 3 m and a 15% target ratio. Ocean and the small waterfall are retained. Landscape/beach/creek borders and surface vertices within 1 m of the original-patch perimeter are locked. Error limits describe meshoptimizer's positional metric, not a proven maximum surface distance. No random sampling, download, texture, position quantization, full-model rewrite or runtime geometry generation occurs. The manifest records layer settings, measured errors, source hashes and counts; decoded buffer-view capacities include padded strides while accessor payload bytes do not. Missing facades and coarser trees are intentional only at overview scale; district/landmark views keep their original full geometry.
- `san-diego-region-source.mjs` acquires the expansion's real OSM coastline, water, road, landcover, and building records as an attributed, reduced gzip snapshot. Explicit `--refresh` downloads bounded tiles, splits exports exceeding the node limit, and obeys service retry delays. Temporary XML tiles live under `/tmp/yuyang-san-diego-region/`.
- `san-diego-region-geometry.mjs` provides shared geographic projection, clipping, terrain-plane draping and batching helpers. The original-patch rectangle derives from the active Torrey Highlands manifest so regional regeneration preserves its complete expanded footprint.
- `san-diego-region-terrain.mjs` authors Torrey Pines' broad meandering crest and tapered, branching ravines, then supplies local approximately 65 m terrain triangles, with approximately 8.125 m cells only near the reserve roads and named trails. Shared boundary subdivisions and a one-cell transition ring meet the original 130 m grid without dangling edges or global refinement strips. Elevation queries, draped surfaces, and foundation splits use these same triangles; exact local vertices and the refinement footprint are recorded in the layout. Small deterministic vertex offsets and alternate diagonals retain flat low-poly shading without texture noise.
- `san-diego-region-roads.mjs` retains exact OSM centerlines and shared junctions while building illustrative connected main/Park Road elevation profiles and restrained contour benches for Guy Fleming, Parry Grove, Beach Trail and the High Point approach. A continuous corridor blend prevents fins where nearby road profiles overlap; narrow local widths use source width/lanes when present. Named hiking trails are moderated separately, preserve mapped steps, and cannot alter the paved cores. Profiles are artistic terrain inputs, not survey data.
- `build-union-square-park.mjs` prepares Union Square park geometry.
- `build-washington-address-index.py` generates the address-to-building index.
- `build-washington-city-tiles.mjs` generates tiled CityGML runtime meshes and manifests, preserves the output folder's existing `INFO.md` guide while clearing stale assets, then regenerates the compact overview and details payload after its final manifest/runtime writes. `npm run data:washington-city-tiles` remains the complete city-asset pipeline.
- `build-washington-park.mjs` prepares Washington Square park geometry.
- `build-washington-planimetrics.mjs` transforms planimetric road and hardscape polygons.
- `build-washington-road-glb.mjs` bakes and compresses the Manhattan roadbed GLB.
- `extract-washington-citygml.py` extracts Washington Square building surfaces from CityGML.
- `san-diego-spawn-utils.mjs` shares source projection, deterministic sampling, spatial checks, population provenance, and output writing between the independent San Diego car and pedestrian generators.
- `info.md` is this folder's guide.
