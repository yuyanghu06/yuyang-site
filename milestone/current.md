## Milestone status

The previous multi-scale atlas and Manhattan camera sequence have been retired. The project is restarting from a single Washington Square study so its visual language can be corrected before any broader website structure is rebuilt.

## Current visual direction

- Render Washington Square and its immediate surrounding neighborhood as a restrained 3D miniature.
- Buildings explicitly selected by the user will eventually receive custom recognizable 3D models.
- All other context buildings use the official NYC 2014 CityGML roof, wall, and ground surfaces with a deterministic, higher-depth sun-faded Village palette spanning limestone (`#B9A98D`), sandstone, dusty brick, terracotta, sage, cool gray stone, aged cream, and dark brown-gray. Roofs are lighter and less saturated than their corresponding walls for clearer massing.
- Three verified buildings bypass the procedural palette through BIN-specific overrides: One Fifth Avenue (`1008847`) uses pale weathered gray stone, the NYU Silver Center complex (`1008820`) uses light gray-beige masonry, and Lipton Hall (`1008875`) retains its earlier muted red-brown brick palette. Each has a distinct roof treatment. Lipton additionally carries 447 dark window instances stacked floor-by-floor across 14 eligible CityGML facade faces.
- Washington Square is populated with 88 deterministic low-poly trees using the exact Courant garden trunk/crown geometry and materials. Placement is validated against the authoritative 37-point OpenStreetMap Washington Square Park boundary: 24 probes around each tree's full scaled crown plus a 0.6-meter safety margin must remain inside the park. Trees also maintain clearance from mapped paths, the fountain, the arch, and neighboring trees, so neither trunks nor canopies enter the surrounding roadbeds.
- Roughly 18% of Washington Square trees are deterministically selected as taller specimens, with height scales between 1.45× and 2×; the remaining trees receive subtler scale variation. Tree footprints are included in pedestrian route validation to prevent trunk clipping.
- Washington Square crowns now vary independently in width, depth, and vertical fullness across a 0.68–1.36 base scale range. Five softly varied Apple-inspired sage greens replace the earlier uniform dark canopy while retaining the shared Courant low-poly tree geometry.
- The mapped fountain footprint now drives a simple low-detail 3D fountain at its exact OSM center and diameter: muted limestone base and rim, recessed blue-gray water, and a small central pedestal/bowl.
- The fountain includes eight lower pale-blue arcing sprays plus a tapered central geyser roughly 1.7 times the mapped basin radius in height, matching the tall dominant plume of the real Washington Square fountain. Animated droplets flow continuously along the side arcs.
- The map now loads `public/models/washington-square-arch.glb`, isolated from the existing Blender Sketchfab scene as nine meshes. All imported materials are overridden with warm white, the model is normalized to the mapped 20.5-meter height, and it uses OSM way `248166269` for its exact north-gate position and rotation. A visibly open procedural model remains only as a load-failure fallback.
- CityGML BIN `1088400` is excluded from context massing because its solid block occupied the same footprint and hid the detailed Blender arch.
- The imported arch material renders double-sided because several Sketchfab cap faces use reversed/single-sided normals; this keeps the upper section visually solid from every camera rotation.
- The approved Washington Square Arch asset now uses the original detailed nine-mesh Sketchfab geometry with one pristine flat stone lid joined directly over its real Blender crown opening. It exports as the single upright `WashingtonArchOriginalFlatTop_Final` object; the earlier simplified corrected copy and all runtime cap/axis workarounds have been removed.
- Mapped crosswalks are being rebuilt from the OSM crossing nodes plus measured planimetric asphalt spans. The new renderer uses thinner, closer muted stripes sized to each street, and the extraction now rejects the erroneous crossings several blocks west of Washington Square.
- Crosswalks now come from tagged OSM `highway=crossing` nodes and inherit their orientation from connected footway geometry; all earlier hand-estimated crossing coordinates were removed.
- Park-path rendering now rejects any segment whose centerline or full-width edge probes enter an official planimetric roadbed. Five longitudinal samples at the center and both sides remove 648 of 1,848 source segments that overlapped asphalt, eliminating the pale bars and starbursts around Union Square road junctions while preserving 1,200 park/sidewalk segments outside roads.
- The downloadable CC BY Sketchfab scene `Washington Square Park v03` by `nyu_grad_alley_2020` is now imported into `assets/blender/nyu-custom-buildings.blend` as the style source for Bobst, Courant, and Stern. Bobst's missing rear wall has been closed with a cleaned mirror of its modeled five-bay front facade, retaining the source masonry, glazing, and opening materials without duplicating side-wall trim.
- Corrected Bobst is exported as the 127 KiB `public/models/bobst-library.glb`, replaces CityGML BIN `1008626`, and is scaled to its authoritative footprint and height. Its yaw follows the long Washington Square South facade edge; a CityGML-only fallback remains for GLB load failure.
- Courant and Stern retain their authoritative CityGML massing instead of receiving separate Blender models. Courant is the narrow BIN `1008627` tower. The grouped Stern selection uses the adjacent white BIN `1078952` and red BIN `1077346` masses. Their CityGML surfaces and procedural facade/roof details are baked into one mesh per interaction target before loading; the background tiles omit those BINs to prevent duplicate geometry. The unrelated BIN `1008629` no longer receives the generated window grid.
- The red/gray Stern palettes are assigned by building identity: 40 West 4th Street uses muted red masonry, while the rotunda-bearing 44–50 West 4th Street mass uses cool gray stone. The rotunda has a curved blue-glass band and a tall purple banner whose top edge attaches to an angled pole and whose full 5.2-unit length hangs directly downward.

The complete plaza composition is rotated `-0.57` radians to run parallel with the nearest official street edge, and its paving is reduced from `29×22` to `26×18` so it remains inside the plaza instead of clipping the road. ## Authoritative future loading and caching architecture

- The finished site will use a single persistent Three.js canvas, WebGL renderer, scene, and React component across its geographic hierarchy. Navigation must not remount Three.js or create a new renderer at each level.
- The intended hierarchy is globe/planetary overview → city view such as Manhattan or San Diego → neighborhood view such as Washington Square or Union Square. The exact globe art direction remains open, but this hierarchy is now the basis for asset-loading and caching work.
- Only the first website startup may use a blocking loading screen. It remains visible while the persistent renderer initializes, initial shaders warm, and the first complete scene is ready. Partial scene geometry must not leak through it. Once ready, the loading screen fades and the initial scene animation begins.
- Every geographic asset group must have three explicit lifecycle states: **downloaded** (bytes in HTTP/browser cache), **prepared** (GLB/data decoded into CPU-side Three.js geometry and materials), and **visible** (objects attached to or revealed in the active scene). Code must not treat a downloaded response as render-ready.
- Versioned GLBs and stable datasets should use durable HTTP caching. Likely next destinations should also be fetched and decoded proactively while the user is viewing the current level; nearby prepared destinations may remain in memory, while distant geometry may be detached/disposed according to a memory budget without invalidating its browser-cached bytes.
- Camera zoom/flight animations are the primary loading window after startup. A globe-to-city flight prepares the city; a city-to-neighborhood flight prepares the selected neighborhood. Hover, selection, scroll intent, and current camera destination should reprioritize the queue before arrival.
- Subsequent geographic transitions must not show another blocking loading screen. If a camera flight reaches its destination before preparation completes, extend or soften the final approach and reveal remaining tiles progressively rather than interrupting the experience with a loader.
- Network fetching, CPU decoding/preparation, GPU attachment, and visual reveal must be independently scheduled. Concurrency must remain bounded, main-thread work must yield, and hidden tabs must stop scheduling new decode/preparation work.
- The current direct-to-Washington prototype and its temporary SVG loading treatment are not the final site architecture. They exist only because the prototype boots directly into its heaviest neighborhood. Future caching work should implement the persistent hierarchy above instead of further polishing the prototype loader.
