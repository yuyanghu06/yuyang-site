# Map runtime

`map-runtime.tsx` composes scene setup, navigation, input, and extracted systems behind the thin `../map.tsx` entry. Its globe view applies a restrained idle spin and parents clouds, planes, boats, and both city markers to the same interactive globe transform. Manhattan loading is owned by `../manhattan/loading/`, hit-testing by `../interaction/`, viewport suspension/start-stop by `viewport-lifecycle.ts`, and resource disposal by `dispose-runtime.ts`.

## Files

- `dispose-runtime.ts` disposes shared geometry, material, texture resources, and InstancedMesh GPU instance buffers when the map unmounts; its object-only cleanup also handles lazy results that finish after teardown. The runtime separately releases the San Diego directional light's shadow targets.
- `INFO.md` is this local ownership guide.
- `landmark-framing.ts` projects all eight merged landmark bounds corners through the destination camera to cap San Diego landmark zoom with a 7% viewport-edge inset. The runtime recalculates this fit on selection and on resize while locked and not manually adjusted; New York presets and the screenshot-centered San Diego overview stay unchanged.
- `san-diego-free-camera.ts` retains the disabled San Diego authoring flag, orthographic orbit/tilt/pan/zoom math, elevation and zoom limits, and displayed-pose JSON serialization. Its flag is false: San Diego uses fixed authored framing and guided inputs, with no authoring controls shown. It has no persistent storage or authored-preset writes.
- `map-runtime.tsx` imports the reorganized agent contracts and JSON-backed camera context, owns the persistent client-side renderer, geographic and landmark view orchestration, validated agent-navigation event handling, settled-view browser notifications, audio-state reporting to the shared site controller, and forwarding of the shell's experience-started gate into the avatar call. It resolves every interactive landmark and its framing from the shared live camera-view registry; agent navigation always uses authored interpolation or the globe cloud flight, and cross-level destinations queue and fully settle at every intermediate camera before advancing instead of cutting camera modes. San Diego is dynamically imported and decoded only on first entry, rendered in a separate scene with matching warm light, mounts its frozen population once after successful scene validation, skips hidden population frame updates, starts staged population reveal on first visible entry, and is held behind the cloud veil until its merged landmark roots are ready. Entry clears the status overlay instead of showing loading text; load failures still display the retry message. All four San Diego destinations have animated clickable pins backed by merged landmark meshes. Mesa Verde and Deer Canyon share hover glow/lift, fixed-angle selection, viewport-fit zoom, history, Escape/scroll-out exit, and guided captions with Westview and the plaza. Failed loads return to the globe with a retry message. City changes route through the globe, landmarks participate in browser history, and scene visibility plus picking filters prevent overlapping local coordinate systems from mixing. Its bottom-right attribution line switches source credit by city and also exposes the unobtrusive internal link to the crawlable blog.
- `viewport-lifecycle.ts` suspends and resumes animation based on page visibility and viewport presence.

Both cities now prepare independently on first entry: fresh globe/San Diego visits do not allocate New York ground, waterfront, roads, tiles, parks, landmarks or population, and fresh New York visits do not fetch San Diego geometry or its city audio. New York dynamically imports its loader/waterfront and shares one first-ready promise until the initial geography and all six landmark registrations finish. Cancelled/superseded loads can populate the cache but cannot replace the current city's status message.

All New York views and San Diego use the shared device quality profile. Touch/coarse-pointer phones and tablets use a city pixel-ratio ceiling of 1.5 with an adaptive floor of 1, a 60 FPS ceiling during interaction and transitions, and the established 30 FPS cadence when settled. San Diego shadows use 1024 pixels on that profile; desktop San Diego retains 2048 and New York retains 1024. City changes, viewport resize and pointer-capability changes refresh quality; adaptive buffer resizing does not restart camera fitting. Shadow-size changes release both old render targets before recreation, and teardown releases both cities' shadow maps. Geometry, population counts and authored cameras remain unchanged.
