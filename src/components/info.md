# React components

Dock placement note: `avatar-call.tsx` consumes the shared agent contracts and tracks the minimized avatar's horizontal and vertical snapped edges so its caption remains inward-facing and aligns with the avatar's bottom edge at either lower corner.

The complete map feature lives under `map/`. Its `map-shell.tsx` is the server-safe dynamic-loading boundary and `map.tsx` is the thin public entry point. Browser-only avatar rendering lives under `avatar-view/`. Markdown-authored camera scripts live under `dialogue/`, grouped into global, Manhattan, Washington Square, and Union Square view families. Component presentation is centralized under `src/styles/`: `avatar-call.tsx`, `agent-chat.tsx`, and `world-loader.tsx` import their dedicated stylesheets there; the loader streams a centered rolling build log before exposing its blue `Dive in` CTA and fading away on activation. `caption-reveal.ts` owns the reusable frame-aligned, elapsed-time-corrected typewriter scheduler used by the chat, while `agent-status.tsx` renders the accessible animated pending-status label. `site-audio.ts` centralizes every browser audio element, playback lifecycle, level, exponential fade transition, and independent global effects/music mute channels shared by the map and dialogue UI.

Keep browser-only Three.js behavior behind the client boundary. `world-loader.tsx` owns production's solid-white animated-globe entry screen and press-to-continue control; its continent drift reverses at both endpoints instead of resetting, and development bypasses the loader. `avatar-view/avatar-fullscreen.tsx` owns the FaceTime modal's shared slightly zoomed-out camera, scene-isolated lighting, increased source-material emission with reduced specular response for smoother but saturated skin, memory-conscious render lifecycle, visibility suspension, post-compile animation start hook, per-frame face-state updates, and guarded responsive renderer synchronization. Live expanded-window resizes and completed expansions update both the WebGL framebuffer and orthographic aspect without a host ResizeObserver or remount. State-specific folders such as `avatar-view/idle/` own their models and animation setup. `avatar-view/avatar-emote-player.ts` expands compact animation-only one-shots against the persistent idle clip without mounting duplicate avatar scenes. `avatar-call.tsx` owns the modal/dock interaction shell, viewport-constrained pointer dragging with nearest-corner snapping and responsive edge margins for the docked tile, and two centered inline circular effects/music mute controls with minimal monochrome SVGs. On desktop those controls exactly match the social buttons' 2.65-rem circle and 0.875-rem icon dimensions, with fixed square sizing that prevents flex distortion. They use the assistant message bubble's dark liquid-glass surface and light foreground, sit directly below the docked avatar, and move to the modal's top-right when expanded. They remain hidden throughout either 460 ms shell transition and fade in only after the corresponding docked or expanded settled state; fixed social controls hide during the expanded avatar state and whenever a docked caption is visible. Desktop retains its approved docked `translate(-59%, -50%) scale(0.34)` composition without forced source dimensions. Mobile uses independent avatar coordinates and scale: docked `translate(-72%, -49%) scale(0.58)` and expanded `translate(-88%, -18%)`, so its framing no longer depends on or mutates desktop positioning. The mobile expanded modal also keeps 1.25 rem of viewport margin on every side, while landscape dialogue can consume the remaining height below the controls instead of collapsing to 42% of a short viewport. Mobile assistant cards explicitly occupy the available width and use normal anywhere wrapping, preventing max-content single-line clipping. The shell also owns its lightweight 12 px CSS backdrop blur, minimized-caption visibility, Escape dismissal, and forwarding of actual visible assistant speech rather than request/reasoning time. Escape marks the caption dismissed before minimizing, preventing it from reappearing beside the docked avatar. The shell rejects drag/open handling for every event originating inside `.agent-chat` and the audio controls, so controls remain interactive without expanding or dragging the tile. It mounts `agent-chat.tsx` only after the avatar has rendered, the experience-start gate is open, and the one-shot startup wave has finished. `agent-chat.tsx` derives Yuyang's displayed age, owns Markdown-authored guided-tour narration, safe HTTP/HTTPS inline-link rendering, capped link-preserving streaming captions, camera navigation, tour/free-mode Next and Back behavior, and an interleaved transcript with right-aligned visitor bubbles. Docked caption positioning is independent of its visible class so completing or advancing a caption fades it out in place without a one-frame horizontal jump. A response that began streaming while expanded is synchronized again when docking settles, keeping its live text visible in the minimized caption.

`site-audio.ts` is the only component module that constructs HTML audio elements. Its shared exponential fade primitive cancels superseded transitions, clamps every interpolated frame to the browser's valid `[0, 1]` volume range, and drives the persistent low-volume four-track background playlist, traffic ambience, building crowd ambience, and both ends of the globe-transition effect; it also owns the singleton dialogue blip and startup priming function. The background playlist starts after the experience gesture, survives all camera/view changes, and crossfades for three seconds between tracks. The map reports view/camera state to its audio controller, while the chat requests throttled blips without manipulating audio elements. An active blip is rewound without issuing redundant `play()` promises. Live agent answers are buffered on arrival and revealed with the same frame-aligned, elapsed-time-corrected local cadence as authored dialogue, preventing slow or bursty network delivery from setting the text and blip rhythm. The renderer batches overdue characters into one React update per painted frame, pauses transcript live-region announcements until the caption completes, and uses resize observation so short-landscape avatar spacing is measured only when caption geometry changes.

The shared dialogue renderer treats every blank-line paragraph break as a mandatory new caption turn, even when the combined text is below the 120-character target. Within each paragraph, it uses a 120-character soft target, prefers a nearby line or punctuation boundary, queues overflow, and reveals one segment per manual blue Next action. While authored dialogue still has unseen segments, one Next control supersedes destination choices, tour navigation, Back, Reply, and every other terminal action; those controls appear only on the final segment. Every phase choice, continuation control, and single Reply trigger otherwise lives inside the latest assistant bubble in both docked and expanded modes. Submitting a mid-response reply clears every unseen queued segment, exits the originating scripted continuation branch, and sends only assistant text already displayed in the transcript as model context. The new model answer then owns its own segmented Next/Done sequence. Guided-tour Next and Back controls continue to navigate between locations until a visitor branches into a freeform reply. While an agent request has no answer text yet, the empty assistant caption displays `Thinking…`; private memory retrieval switches it live to `Remembering…`, Tavily search switches it to `Researching…`, and composition switches it back to `Thinking…`. All three labels animate as a subtle wave across every character, with motion disabled for visitors who prefer reduced motion. These transient states never enter the transcript. The final white Done presentation pill minimizes the guide; expanded Reply opens its inline composer in the same bubble.

Docked captions are persistent across outside clicks, fullscreen expansion, and later re-minimization. The explicit × at the top-left dismisses one directly, and every minimized action pill fades the current bubble closed before running its action; maximizing the call never clears caption visibility.

Caption visibility is gated to the shell's complete 460 ms mode transition in both directions. On minimize, the docked bubble remains hidden until the avatar tile settles. On expansion, an explicit `expandedSettled` state holds the fullscreen caption at zero opacity for the complete resize, then starts its 220 ms fade after the modal reaches final size; this prevents the previous docked opacity from carrying into the expanded frame. New streams that begin after the dock settles still reveal immediately, while streams during the transition wait for the same gate.

Minimized action pills—including Yes/No, Okay, destinations, Next, and presentation controls—first fade out the current caption over 180 ms, then run their action. Reply remains visible because it edits the current bubble instead of advancing it. The initial introduction follows the same unfinished-segment priority as other authored dialogue: only Next appears while another caption segment remains, and Yes/No appear after the final segment. Click and keyboard events from the chat are stopped before reaching the dock shell, so opening or submitting the inline composer cannot expand the minimized avatar. Composer-open state overrides every phase-specific input gate. On the active initial choice caption, opening Reply keeps Yes/No fixed at the left and replaces the single Reply trigger with a flexible composer sized to the remaining width in the same row. Once an agent answer owns a presentation action, its composer renders normally even if the conversation originated from that intro choice. A queued or streamed follow-up explicitly reveals its new caption after the previous bubble has closed.

Expanded phase-specific Reply rendering yields to the terminal presentation Reply branch whenever `presentationAction` is `reply`, ensuring the latest caption can never display duplicate Reply triggers on mobile or desktop.

The expanded history re-applies its latest-caption scroll anchor whenever the call expands, because docked mode intentionally removes the invisible scroll room and cannot establish the expanded default position. Control-state changes also refresh that anchor so the newest card and its pill remain the base view while older turns stay available by scrolling upward.

When only one assistant caption exists, the scroll range is fixed at zero so the initial message stays in its natural unshifted position with its controls beneath it. Latest-caption anchoring begins only after a real multi-message history exists.

The Washington narration is also bound to the settled Washington camera event for scroll-driven entry. It may start once from the expected Washington-arrival phase, the Manhattan destination-choice phase, or free exploration; a ref prevents replay on later returns. The guided Union Square branch runs from the full-area view to Carlyle Court at 25 Union Square West and then Shift at 235 Park Avenue South. Guided-tour visits track Union Square and Washington Square independently. Finishing either complete location branch offers a named move-on action to the other's full-area view only when that location has not already been visited during the tour; choosing it marks the destination visited before navigation, so completing the second branch cannot create an endless loop. Visitors may end the tour from the same prompt. Direct landmark clicks still stream the corresponding Markdown narration with capped Next and free-mode Back behavior. Declining the tour and unrelated active scripted phases are not interrupted.

In short landscape, the dialogue renderer observes the visible latest card, message list, and direct controls for actual size changes, then measures them once on the next animation frame and publishes their bottom edge as `--expanded-avatar-top`. The avatar stylesheet clamps that live coordinate to safe framing bounds, keeping a consistent gap as captions wrap or actions change without forcing layout for every streamed character; effect cleanup disconnects the observer, cancels its pending frame, and removes the property.
