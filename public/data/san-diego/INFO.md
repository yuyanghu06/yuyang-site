# San Diego geographic data

Static, reproducible OpenStreetMap geography for the San Diego region and its Torrey Highlands, Torrey Pines, and Park Village views. The original Camino del Sur / SR-56 asset remains separate and unchanged. The expansion covers Solana Beach/Fairbanks Ranch, UCSD/Miramar, the coast, and Carmel Mountain/Scripps Ranch. All local coordinates are meters, east +X and north −Z, relative to longitude −117.1518, latitude 32.9606.

There are no direct subfolders.

- `source.osm.json` is the compact OpenStreetMap source export with original feature IDs, tagged footprints, roads, landcover, source trees, and Chef Zhu's mapped restaurant point. It is licensed under ODbL 1.0, with attribution to OpenStreetMap contributors.
- `manifest.json` records geographic bounds, projection, source timestamp, Map3D commit, output hash, counts, assumptions, camera framing, merged landmark source IDs, the sage/cream/terracotta palette, and the lossless position-compression contract that preserves surface separation.
- `layout.json` retains projected building/road/tree layout and exclusion polygons for reproducibility and independent geometry-placement audits.
- `region-source.osm.json.gz` is the expansion's reduced, deduplicated real OSM snapshot, including source feature IDs, coordinate geometry, relevant tags, acquisition bounds, and license. It supports ordinary offline regeneration; decompression yields JSON with `elements` and provenance.
- `region-manifest.json` records the expansion's GLB hash, metric/geographic bounds, projection, source provenance, regional overview, three district camera/bounds records, original semantic palette, feature counts, original-model dependency, and explicit artistic assumptions. Its authoritative `coverageBounds` uses the mapped source rectangle and measured full-content height range, keeping a conservative lower limit of Y=-5 for stable camera framing, independently of the peripheral skirt and overview fitting bounds; `groundHeightRange` records the terrain alone. Surface metadata explains shoreline topology, shared triangle planes, semantic separation and the flat original-patch seam. Its waterfall annotation is static Park Village scenery, never a separate destination; the waterfall metadata also records its actual generated bounds and dimensions.
- `region-layout.json` records the retained regional building source IDs/centers/heights and foundation ranges, exact terrain-grid axes, supplemental tree positions/canopy radii, tree-exclusion source IDs, coastline and waterway IDs, mapped named features, counts, and the waterfall's verified OSM node location for asset audits.
- `MAP3D-LICENSE.txt` preserves the upstream Map3D MIT copyright and license notice.
- `INFO.md` is this folder guide.

Generate with `node scripts/map-data/build-san-diego.mjs`; add `--refresh` only to request a new OSM snapshot. The ignored checkout at `data/work/map3d/` is pinned to commit `2c5d732477ba7c55995572aae926bf80303c00b9`. The generator executes its `Space.tsx` building-extrusion implementation, using metric projection and documented height inputs. Terrain elevation, inferred heights, facade details, and supplemental planting are stylized assumptions; footprints and street layout are mapped data.

Generate the separate expansion with `npm run data:san-diego-region`. Its `--refresh` flag explicitly reacquires OSM exports; ordinary runs use the compressed snapshot. The expanded model has an exact rectangular exclusion for the original patch and does not rewrite `source.osm.json`, `manifest.json`, `layout.json`, or the original GLB. Coarse regional building sampling preserves all focus-area footprints and large buildings. Coastal/canyon elevation is an artistic interpretation, not a surveyed height model. The photograph-inspired waterfall uses OSM node `1863363847` at latitude `32.926841`, longitude `-117.1773305`; its small angular boulders sit in the local ground beside the mapped creek, with a connected short cascade and pool. The formation follows the creek direction, with low 2–4.25 m rock outcrops and restrained foam. The twenty rocks retain their centers, colors and rotations, with reduced horizontal radii and 0.8 m of terrain embed; water, pool and foam retain their existing geometry. Its existing label remains visible in the base Park Village view.

All regional surface layers share a 130 m terrain grid with additional lines on the exact original-patch perimeter. Terrain meets the original ground at Y=-0.03; semantic surface offsets blend to the original flat heights over 260 m. Landcover uses deterministic semantic heights, and roads/streams sit above those surfaces. The connected mainland coastline and five closed tidal-island rings define both the Pacific surface and the land clipping, preserving the San Dieguito estuary's separate banks. A documented peripheral terrain/ocean skirt exists outside source coverage, but camera containment uses the mapped rectangle rather than that undetailed margin.

Building wall bottoms split wherever the terrain changes plane and extend 0.08 m below it. Level roofs clear the highest point within each footprint. Parking, sports, playgrounds, water, all mapped streets and all mapped buildings exclude supplemental planting even when their smaller regional geometry is omitted by LOD. The manifest records these rules, bridge approaches, footprint/road simplification, coarse sampling, surface heights, and lossless position compression.

Torrey Pines deliberately exaggerates its coastal reserve into an approximately 300 m ridge for a readable mountain silhouette. The long crest, steep sandstone-colored western cliff facets, eastward descent and diagonal gullies taper into the existing terrain. The cliff uses one existing warm rock color so actual face angles supply its shading. A gently sloping terrain shoulder beneath two small reserve service buildings avoids oversized foundation walls. The beach and lagoon retain their lower terrain, and the model's shared elevation field carries trails, landcover, trees and building foundations over the ridge. Only Torrey Pines receives the oblique mountain-view camera. The relief settings and camera angle are recorded in `torreyPinesRelief`; this is artistic illustration rather than surveyed elevation or a DEM.
